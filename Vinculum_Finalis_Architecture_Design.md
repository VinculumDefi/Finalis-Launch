## A. System component map

Implementation class tags: **[BASE-CHAIN]** (Base, Solidity/EVM), **[SOURCE-ENV]** (per-environment native program/contract/script/operation), **[OFF-CHAIN]** (off-chain process), **[APP]** (public application), **[DEPLOY]** (deployment/finalization), **[VERIFY]** (test/verification).

### A.1 Source-environment Commitment Vault mechanisms — `SRC-LOCK` [SOURCE-ENV], per environment
- **Responsibilities:** Accept an approved external asset in its **original native or token form** (two explicit custody paths where applicable — see Section L); enforce permitted durations and multipliers; compute and route the actual rounded fee; isolate remaining principal until maturity; bind User, release destination, selected output token, and Base recipient; bind Valuation Timestamp and price record; emit a normalized lock event with a unique Commitment Vault Lock identifier; **enforce the Trust-Building Handshake allowance per objectively bound source-chain identity according to the selected source mechanism's chain-native state capability** (one or three successful qualifying Handshakes; Section N); enforcement may be on the source environment (programmable, persistent per-identity state) and/or in the Base proof-verification path (non-stateful); **hold a permanent CHONX-activation flag set only by a verified Base activation message** (Section H), or prove activation via the proof-verification causal path on non-programmable chains.
- **Permitted authority:** None discretionary. Accept or reject only.
- **Stored state:** Lock record (unique lock id, source env id, canonical asset identity, gross amount, actual fee amount + asset, principal, creation timestamp, maturity, selected output token, Base recipient, release destination, Valuation Timestamp, price-record binding, Handshake identity + per-identity allowance counter where source-enforceable, CHONX-activation flag/receipt).
- **Inputs:** User selection, registry identity, price record, fee basis points, multiplier table, asset-precision metadata (deployment dependency, Section I.6).
- **Outputs:** Chain-native atomic fee transfer + principal custody (or, on non-programmable chains, a chain-native construction producing the equivalent completed disposition — Section Q); normalized lock event/evidence; Handshake allowance decrement where source-enforceable (otherwise recorded at recognition, Section N).
- **Dependencies:** `SRC-FEE`, `SRC-BIND`, `SRC-FIN`, `BASE-REG` (identity), `PRICE-DELIVER` (price reference), `BASE-ACT` (activation-state reference), `BASE-QNORM` (precision reference).

### A.2 Original-form fee routing — `SRC-FEE` [SOURCE-ENV]
- Unchanged (100% of actual rounded fee in the original asset to the environment's fixed Dev Fund destination; original form preserved).

### A.3 Principal-release mechanisms — `SRC-RELEASE` [SOURCE-ENV]
- Unchanged (release at maturity, User-initiated, exactly once, independent of Base and every external dependency; no early/cancel/admin/oracle release). The XRPL `EscrowCancel` early-cancellation conflict is recorded in Section C.10 / J.13; the recognized XRPL design must remove the early-cancel path.

### A.4 User and release-destination binding — `SRC-BIND` [SOURCE-ENV]
- Unchanged, with the clarification that on non-programmable environments (UTXO, XRPL, Stellar native ops) the binding is carried by the transaction itself (script/claimant/memo), and the **source chain binds it at creation within the chain-native construction**. An arbitrary transaction constructed outside the protocol does not become a Commitment Vault Lock or create Vinculum rights merely because it moves or timelocks assets (Section Q); the binding is verified at recognition (Section M).

### A.5 Price fetcher — `PRICE-FETCH` [OFF-CHAIN]
- Unchanged.

### A.6 Signed/batched price delivery — `PRICE-DELIVER` [OFF-CHAIN + BASE-CHAIN receiver]
- Unchanged and **distinct from source-lock evidence**.

### A.7 Normalized cross-chain evidence production — `SRC-EVID` [SOURCE-ENV + OFF-CHAIN]
- **Responsibilities:** Produce a normalized proof binding the completed source event and all VF-XCH-011 fields, the **RAC identity** from immutable source facts (Section D), the **CHONX-activation receipt** (Section H), and the **Handshake identity** (Section N/Q.2).
- **Permitted authority:** Produce evidence only. No approval authority.
- **Dependencies:** `SRC-FIN`, `SRC-FEE`, `PRICE-DELIVER`, `BASE-ACT`.

### A.8 Evidence transport — `RELAY` [OFF-CHAIN]
- Unchanged (transport-only, permissionless; a relayer signature does not establish truth).

### A.9 Base verification and replay protection — `BASE-VERIFY` [BASE-CHAIN]
- **Responsibilities:** Validate source finality, registry identity, fee routing, price record, output eligibility, recipient binding, replay protection, **CHONX-activation receipt + causal ordering** (Section H), **asset-quantity normalization** (Section P), and **Handshake identity allowance consumption** (one or three uses per identity; Section N) where not source-enforced. Replay consumption of (env, lock id) is decoupled from Reward-Accounting Credit recording (Repair 1.A). `BASE-VERIFY` is the recognition boundary: only a qualifying source event that satisfies the complete recognized source-event design becomes a Commitment Vault Lock and may authorize issuance (Section Q.4.1).
- **Stored state:** Consumed (env, lock id) set; RAC-identity set (immutable-facts key); per-identity Handshake allowance counter (where not source-enforced), keyed by the bound source-chain identity and the applicable allowance (one or three).
- **Outputs:** Verified-or-rejected issuance decision; on successful fee verification, a Reward-Accounting Credit (unless VCLM lifetime capacity already zero); on successful issuance, mint trigger.

### A.10 Approved Asset Registry — `BASE-REG` [BASE-CHAIN + APP]
- Unchanged (1,001 immutable records per Appendix C; VF-REG-011 conformity; registry fields not modified). Asset-precision metadata is a separate deployment dependency (Section I.6), not a registry field. The current governing registry already assigns row 54 BCH to Bitcoin Cash (Section K).

### A.11 Issuance calculations — `BASE-ISSUE` + `BASE-EMIT` + `BASE-MULT` [BASE-CHAIN]
- Unchanged (separate from fee verification / Reward-Accounting Credit).

### A.12 Global lifetime-cap accounting — `BASE-CAP` [BASE-CHAIN]
- Unchanged.

### A.13 CHONX and SYNTH activation gates — `BASE-ACT` [BASE-CHAIN]
- **Responsibilities (Repair 2):** record the Base block/timestamp at which cumulative lifetime VCLM first reached 10,000,000 (CHONX activation point) and at which cumulative lifetime CHONX first reached 100,000,000 (SYNTH activation point). **`BASE-ACT` emits a verifiable activation message** (signed/stated on Base) consumable by source environments to set their permanent CHONX-activation flag (Section H). `BASE-VERIFY` uses the CHONX activation point and the source activation-receipt evidence to evaluate VF-COM-025. On non-programmable chains, the causal activation-at-creation relationship is established via the proof-verification path (Section H/Q), not by a mutable on-chain flag.

### A.14–A.15 Token components and SYNTH Forge — `BASE-TOK-*`, `BASE-FORGE`
- Unchanged.

### A.16 Treasury Reward Stake and epoch accounting — `BASE-STAKE` + `BASE-EPOCH` + `BASE-RAC` [BASE-CHAIN]
- **`BASE-RAC` (Repair 1.A + Repair 2.K):** records a Reward-Accounting Credit upon successful fee verification (not upon issuance success), exactly once per **RAC identity derived from immutable source facts** (Section D), assigned to the epoch in which the fee is successfully recorded on Base; after VCLM lifetime capacity reaches zero, later fees create no credit. Credit recording is independent of issuance replay consumption.
- **`BASE-EPOCH` (Repair 1.C):** two-phase (close at scheduled end of N; allocate after scheduled end of N+1; eligibility fixed across N start → N+1 end). Delayed processing never changes scheduled boundaries or eligibility timestamps; pending processing chronological.

### A.17 Axelar ITS — `AXELAR-ITS`
- Unchanged.

### A.18 Deployment finalization and authority termination — `DEPLOY-FIN` + `DEPLOY-MANIFEST`
- Unchanged, plus: finalization verifies and records asset-precision metadata (Section P) and the CHONX/SYNTH activation recording is live on Base and the source CHONX-activation flags/receipts are wired to verified Base activation messages or proof-verification causal paths.

### A.19 Public application boundary — `APP-PUBLIC` [APP]
- Unchanged (read/display only; no economic promises).

### A.20 Asset-quantity normalization — `BASE-QNORM` [BASE-CHAIN + DEPLOY]
- Unchanged (immutable asset-precision table; precision never from User/relayer). Per-family normalization and boundary calculations in Section P.

### A.21 Source-side preflight — `SRC-PREFLIGHT` [SOURCE-ENV + BASE-VERIFY]
- **Responsibilities (Repair 2 + Repair 3 + Repair 4):** ensure that only a qualifying source event can become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock and authorize Base issuance. CODA may place deterministic checks at the source, in a cryptographic authorization required by the source mechanism, in the proof-verification path, or across a chain-native combination (Section Q.4). Preflight verifies, as applicable to each environment: immutable approved asset identity, authenticated asset precision, the signed Universal Price Fetcher v9 record, selected duration and multiplier, minimum-USD / Handshake value range, the exact rounded fee, CHONX availability at creation, and the bound Base recipient. **A User's ability to create an arbitrary raw transaction is not a protocol-preflight bypass**: such a transaction creates no Vinculum rights unless it satisfies the complete recognized source-event design; the proof-verification path rejects every non-qualifying event. **A website or transaction builder alone is not source-chain enforcement**, but source-chain enforcement is no longer the only permitted preflight location (Section M). Status terminology: a named CLTV pattern, batch, verifier, counter, module, or proof path is not, by itself, a deployable mechanism — see Section Q.7 and the per-environment statuses.

## B. End-to-end state transitions

### B.1 Trust-Building Handshake (Repair 1.B + Repair 2.N + Repair 3 + Repair 4 — one-versus-three rule)
`SELECT (1h, qualifying asset, value $0.95–$1.05)` → `SOURCE_PREFLIGHT (per-identity allowance > 0 for the applicable count, output availability, registry, amount, price, recipient, CHONX-activation receipt if CHONX output)` → **`SOURCE_NATIVE_TX`: in the environment's chain-native atomic construction — (a) compute fee (2.50%, floor; reject if fee=0 or principal=0), (b) route fee to Dev Fund, (c) lock principal (1h), (d) bind fields + lock id + Handshake identity, (e) consume one identity-key allowance** → `SOURCE_FINALITY` → `EVIDENCE` → `BASE_VERIFY (fee verification + issuance capacity check + Handshake identity allowance check)` → `ISSUE (VCLM or activated CHONX) | REJECT_ISSUANCE`.
- **One-versus-three Handshake rule (Repair 4 — owner-approved; Section Q.2):** the Trust-Building Handshake allowance now depends on the selected source mechanism's chain-native state capability. A source mechanism capable of atomically maintaining persistent per-identity allowance state permits **three** successful qualifying Handshakes per objectively bound source-chain identity. A source mechanism without that capability permits **one** successful qualifying Handshake per objectively bound source-chain identity. The applicable allowance is determined by the actual selected source mechanism, not by a broad chain label.
- **Settled Handshake identity (Repair 3 — Section Q.2):** the allowance is per **objectively recorded source-chain identity**, not per human being. Each distinct source wallet or account (account-model chains) or canonical release public key (UTXO-family mechanisms, R.13) receives its own applicable allowance (one or three). A person may create another wallet, account, or release public key and receive its own applicable allowance; this is **permitted and is not a bypass, security defect, Sybil issue, or prohibited behavior**. No wallet clustering, person-level identification, cross-address association, detection of common control, or anti-Sybil enforcement is attempted.
- **Under the currently selected designs:** the seven EVM environments and Solana (persistent on-chain per-identity state) permit **three** successful Handshakes per bound identity. Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dogecoin, DigiByte, transparent Zcash, and Bitcoin Cash (no persistent on-source per-identity state) permit **one** successful Handshake per bound **canonical release public key**. `handshake_identity = (source_environment_id, canonical_release_public_key)` — the single public key authorizing the principal's maturity-release branch, serialized in the chain's canonical public-key form and bound/proved against the principal output's locking script, redeem/witness script, or tapleaf evidence. The address, complete script, script hash, and maturity value are not used as alternative identities. Handshake-compatible UTXO outputs must expose exactly one unambiguous maturity-release public key; ambiguous or multi-key constructions are rejected for Handshake use. A newly generated release public key remains permitted and receives its own one-use allowance. XRPL native and Stellar native-operation designs permit **one** successful Handshake per bound source account unless CODA establishes and selects a genuinely deployable source-enforced stateful mechanism (in which case three). Cosmos Hub's allowance remains dependent on resolution of its source mechanism.
- The allowance is consumed **at source completion** (recognized event). **For a one-use mechanism:** the first successful qualifying Handshake consumes the identity's allowance; a second qualifying attempt by the same identity cannot become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock or authorize issuance; a newly created distinct identity may complete its own one permitted Handshake. **For a three-use mechanism:** the first three successful qualifying Handshakes consume the identity's allowance; the fourth qualifying attempt by the same identity is rejected. **A failed, reverted, invalid, or unrecognized attempt consumes no allowance**; an arbitrary transaction outside the protocol creates no Vinculum rights. No standard-fee one-hour fallback exists.

### B.2 Standard Commitment Vault Lock
Unchanged; preflight per Section M; chain-native atomic construction per Section L/C.

### B.3 Successful Base issuance (Repair 1.A)
`EVIDENCE_RECEIVED` → `VERIFY (...)` → (1) `FEE_VERIFIED` → record Reward-Accounting Credit (exact-once by **RAC identity from immutable source facts**, Section D) assigned to current epoch [independent of issuance outcome]; (2) `CAPACITY_FIT` → `CALC` → `MINT` → `CONSUME (env+lock id for issuance replay)`.

### B.4–B.9
Unchanged from Repair 1 (failed proof / corrected resubmission; permanent issuance failure; principal maturity; cap rejection; CHONX activation recording; SYNTH activation and forging).

### B.10 Reward-Accounting Credit recording (Repair 1.A + Repair 2.K)
`FEE_VERIFIED_ON_BASE` → `dedup by RAC identity = (source_env_id, lock_id, canonical_fee_tx_hash, fee_op_or_output_index, canonical_fee_asset, actual_fee_amount)` → if not already recorded and VCLM capacity > 0 → `RAC = Verified USD Fee Value × 0.60` → `ASSIGN to current epoch`. The identity is derived from immutable source facts, **not from serialized proof bytes**, so equivalent proofs / corrected encodings / retries produce the same key.

### B.11 Epoch finalization, allocation, accumulation, claim (Repair 1.C)
Unchanged two-phase (close at end of N; allocate after end of N+1; eligibility start-of-N → end-of-N+1; single mint to immutable `BASE-STAKE`; zero-eligible and capacity-rejection mark credits Used; delayed processing never shifts boundaries; chronological pending).

### B.12–B.13
Unchanged (Treasury Reward Stake extension; Axelar ITS transport).

### B.14 Asset-quantity normalization flow (Repair 1.G + Repair 2.P)
`LOCK_EVIDENCE (gross in smallest units + canonical asset identity)` → `BASE-QNORM resolves precision from immutable asset-precision table` → `convert smallest units → asset units` → `Verified Gross USD Value = asset units × signed/batched price` → fee/issuance. Rounding boundaries and overflow bounds in Section P.

### B.15 CHONX activation-at-creation check (Repair 2.H / Repair 3 — causal ordering, established via source flag or proof-verification path)
`BASE activates CHONX permanently at B_act` → `BASE-ACT emits verifiable activation message` → `source mechanism accepts activation message and sets permanent CHONX-activation flag (programmable), OR the proof-verification path establishes the causal relationship (non-programmable)` → `only a causally-later source event may create a CHONX-output lock` → `evidence proves (a) the activation receipt and (b) the later source-chain ordering`. See Section H. Fails closed if the causal relationship cannot be objectively established.

## G. Security and trust model

- **Removed (Repair 1.D, retained):** fixed project-appointed signer set. No reporter committee, multisig, administrator, or discretionary validator group. Relayer is transport-only; its signature is not truth. Price delivery remains the established signed/batched path, distinct from source-lock evidence.
- **Chain-equivalent outcome principle (Repair 3 — Section Q.1):** the protocol requires equivalent outcomes, security, and economic performance across environments; it does not require identical contracts, transaction structures, state models, or implementation methods. A chain-native design is evaluated by whether it produces the required observable result (Section Q.3 invariants), not by resemblance to an EVM contract.
- **One-versus-three Handshake rule (Repair 4 — Section Q.2):** the allowance depends on the selected source mechanism's chain-native state capability (three for persistent per-identity state; one otherwise), applied per objectively bound source-chain identity, not per human. Each distinct identity receives its own applicable allowance; creating a new wallet/account/release public key is permitted and is not a bypass, security defect, or Sybil issue. No wallet clustering, person-level identification, cross-address association, detection of common control, or anti-Sybil enforcement is performed.
- **Per-environment verifiable proof paths (Repair 2.O + Repair 3/4):** each environment identifies an objectively verifiable proof path; where no deployable on-Base verifier is established, the path is DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED. Native assets are not represented as ERC-20/SPL-token unless Appendix C identifies the wrapped form.
- **Source-side preflight (Repair 2.M + Repair 3/4):** preflight ensures only a qualifying source event becomes a recognized Commitment Vault Lock. A User's ability to create an arbitrary raw transaction is not a protocol bypass — such a transaction creates no Vinculum rights unless it satisfies the complete recognized source-event design. Deterministic checks may be at the source, in a cryptographic authorization, in the proof-verification path, or across a chain-native combination (Section Q.4).

- **RAC exact-once (Repair 1.A + Repair 2.K):** keyed by immutable source facts, independent of issuance replay and of proof encoding.

## H. CHONX activation existed at source creation time (Repair 2 + Repair 3 + Repair 4 — causal ordering, established via source flag or proof-verification path)

VF-COM-025 requires CHONX to have been active at the original Commitment Vault Lock creation time; later activation cannot cure an earlier ineligible selection. Independent Base and source-chain timestamps are **not** compared, and no clock-skew tolerance is invented. Causal source-chain ordering is used:

1. Base permanently activates CHONX at block `B_act` (`BASE-ACT` records it).
2. `BASE-ACT` emits a **verifiable Base activation message** (signed/stated on Base, consumable cross-chain).
3. The source mechanism accepts the activation message only after verifying it against Base state, and either sets a **permanent CHONX-activation flag** (programmable) or the proof-verification path establishes the causal relationship from source-chain block ordering (non-programmable).
4. Only a **later** source event (causally after the activation) may create a CHONX-output Commitment Vault Lock; a CHONX-output selection before activation is rejected.
5. Evidence proves **both** (a) the activation receipt (the verified Base activation message + the source flag-setting transaction, or the proof-verification equivalent) and (b) the later source-chain ordering (the lock event causally follows the activation on the source chain).

**Per-environment Base→source state path (Repair 3/4):**
- **Programmable (EVM, Solana):** a permanent activation flag in the source contract/program, set by a verified Base activation message delivered via Axelar ITS / a verified Base-state oracle / an L1-anchored message. The contract/program rejects CHONX-output locks until the flag is set; the flag is set only by the verified message. The exact Base→source verified-message delivery mechanism is a deployment dependency — DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED per environment until a concrete deployable channel is established.
- **Non-programmable (Bitcoin/Litecoin/Dogecoin/DigiByte/Zcash/Bitcoin Cash UTXO, XRPL native, Stellar native ops):** there is no mutable on-chain state to hold a permanent activation flag in the lock mechanism itself. ## N. Trust-Building Handshake enforcement (Repair 4 — one-versus-three rule)

A source mechanism capable of atomically maintaining persistent per-identity allowance state permits **three** successful qualifying Handshakes per objectively bound source-chain identity. A source mechanism without that capability permits **one** successful qualifying Handshake per objectively bound source-chain identity. ## P. Asset-quantity normalization (per family + boundary calculations)

Immutable asset-precision metadata bound to canonical asset identity at deployment finalization; verified by reading each asset's on-chain decimals/mint/denom metadata; recorded in the manifest; never supplied by User/relayer. Conflicting metadata is rejected.

**Conversion formula:** `asset_units = gross_smallest_units / 10^precision`; `Verified Gross USD Value = asset_units × signed/batched price`. Overflow bounds: intermediate `gross_smallest_units × price` computed in 256-bit fixed-point; `asset_units` capped at the asset's total supply bound; the architecture rejects any quantity whose normalization overflows the 18-decimal issuance arithmetic. Rounding boundaries: USD value computed at full precision, then **floor** at the fee integer division `floor(gross × bps / 10000)` (VF-COM-011) and at each issuance integer division (VF-COM-019); never round up; never reorder.

**Representative boundary calculations:**
- Native BTC, gross = 0.00100000 BTC (100000 sat), price = $30,000.00, fee 5.00%: `asset_units = 100000 / 10^8 = 0.001`; `Verified Gross USD = 0.001 × 30000 = $30.00 ≥ $10.00 min`; `fee_units = floor(100000 × 500 / 10000) = 5000 sat = 0.00005 BTC`; `principal = 100000 − 5000 = 95000 sat`. (Handshake value range checked analogously against $0.95–$1.05.)
- EVM token (6-decimal stablecoin), gross = 20,000,000 units (20.00), price = $1.00, fee 5.00%: `Verified Gross USD = $20.00`; `fee_units = floor(20000000 × 500 / 10000) = 1000000 units = 1.00`; `principal = 19000000 units`.
- Solana SPL (9-decimal), gross = 2_000_000_000 units (2.0), price = $5.00, fee 5.00%: `Verified Gross USD = $10.00 ≥ $10.00`; `fee_units = floor(2e9 × 500 / 10000) = 100000000 units = 0.1`; `principal = 1_900_000_000`.
- Native XRP, gross = 50_000_000 drops (50.00 XRP), price = $0.60, fee 5.00%: `Verified Gross USD = $30.00`; `fee_units = floor(50000000 × 500 / 10000) = 2500000 drops = 2.5 XRP`; `principal = 47_500_000 drops`.
- Native XLM, gross = 200_000_000 stroops (20.00 XLM), price = $0.10, fee 5.00%: `Verified Gross USD = $2.00 < $10.00 → REJECT (below standard minimum)`; demonstrates the minimum-USD boundary on a native asset.

Precision metadata does not cure an incorrect contract, mint, environment, or custody path (Section K): a verified decimal value is meaningful only against the verified canonical asset identity and custody path.

## Q. Chain-equivalent outcome principle, settled Handshake identity, and one-versus-three rule (Repair Pass 3 + Pass 4 — owner direction)

### Q.1 Chain-equivalent outcome principle
The protocol requires **equivalent outcomes, security, and economic performance** across environments. It does **not** require identical contracts, transaction structures, state models, or implementation methods. CODA is authorized to select and document a different chain-native mechanism for each environment without requesting an owner decision merely because the mechanism differs from EVM. A chain-specific design is **not** rejected because it differs from an EVM contract; it is evaluated by whether it produces the required observable result.

### Q.2 Settled Trust-Building Handshake identity interpretation + one-versus-three rule (Repair 4)
The Trust-Building Handshake allowance now depends on the **selected source mechanism's chain-native state capability**:
- A source mechanism capable of atomically maintaining persistent per-identity allowance state permits **three** successful qualifying Trust-Building Handshakes per objectively bound source-chain identity.
- A source mechanism without that capability permits **one** successful qualifying Trust-Building Handshake per objectively bound source-chain identity.
The applicable allowance is determined by the actual selected source mechanism, not by a broad chain label.

The allowance is per **objectively recorded source-chain identity**, not per human being. Each distinct source wallet or account (account-model chains) or canonical release public key (UTXO-family mechanisms, R.13) receives its own applicable allowance (one or three). A person may create another wallet, account, or release public key and receive its own applicable allowance; this is **permitted and is not a bypass, security defect, Sybil issue, or prohibited behavior**. CODA must **not** attempt person-level identification, wallet clustering, cross-address association, detection of common control, or anti-Sybil enforcement. The only required limit is that the same bound Handshake identity cannot complete more than its applicable allowance of qualifying Handshakes. Failed, reverted, invalid, or unrecognized attempts consume no allowance. An arbitrary transaction constructed outside the protocol does not become a Commitment Vault Lock or create protocol rights merely because it moves or timelocks assets. CODA may define the objective Handshake identity per chain according to that chain's native account or state model, and for UTXO-family mechanisms must use exactly one canonical release public key (R.13); in every case it must bind the identity deterministically into the recognized lock evidence. Use "successful qualifying Trust-Building Handshake per objectively bound source-chain identity," not the imprecise phrase "test per human wallet."

Under the currently selected designs: the seven EVM environments and Solana (persistent on-chain per-identity state) permit three successful Handshakes per bound identity. Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dogecoin, DigiByte, transparent Zcash, and Bitcoin Cash (no persistent on-source per-identity state) permit one successful Handshake per bound canonical release public key (`handshake_identity = (source_environment_id, canonical_release_public_key)` — R.13: the single public key authorizing the principal's maturity-release branch; the address, complete script, script hash, and maturity value are not alternative identities; ambiguous or multi-key maturity-release paths are rejected for Handshake use; a newly generated release public key is permitted and receives its own one-use allowance). XRPL native and Stellar native-operation designs permit one successful Handshake per bound source account unless CODA establishes and selects a genuinely deployable source-enforced stateful mechanism (in which case three). Cosmos Hub's allowance remains dependent on resolution of its source mechanism.

### Q.3 Mandatory outcomes and invariants
Every chain-specific mechanism must preserve these outcomes (equivalent protection is required; identical machinery is not):
1. A recognized Commitment Vault Lock is objectively bound to its source environment, approved asset, amount, actual fee, principal, source identity, maturity, output token, Base recipient, release destination, valuation record, and unique replay identifier.
2. The protocol fee and principal disposition cannot create a protocol-recognized partial or contradictory lock state.
3. Principal remains on its required source environment and has a deterministic maturity-release path.
4. Principal release cannot depend on Base issuance, a relayer, price service, administrator, verifier availability, or later external cooperation.
5. Base issuance requires deterministic verification of a valid finalized source event.
6. One recognized source lock authorizes no more than one issuance.
7. One verified source fee authorizes no more than one applicable Reward-Accounting Credit.
8. No relayer, website, administrator, project representative, or discretionary approver can make an invalid event valid.
9. Lifetime caps, calculations, multipliers, durations, fee rates, activation thresholds, output restrictions, and token economics remain unchanged.
10. Failure must be fail-closed for new issuance without endangering matured principal.

### Q.4 Functional interpretation of atomicity and preflight
Atomicity means the chain-specific mechanism cannot produce a protocol-recognized partial completion that violates the required fee, principal, binding, or release outcome. A compliant mechanism may use: one transaction with multiple outputs; one transaction with multiple operations; a native atomic batch; a deterministic chain-native construction; source checks; cryptographic authorization; the proof-verification path; or a documented combination of these. A User's ability to create an arbitrary raw transaction is not a protocol-preflight bypass: such a transaction creates no Vinculum rights unless it satisfies the complete recognized source-event design. Preflight must ensure that only a qualifying source event can become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock and authorize Base issuance. CODA may place deterministic checks at the source, in a cryptographic authorization required by the source mechanism, in the proof-verification path, or across a chain-native combination, provided the resulting User-protection and security outcome are demonstrated.

### Q.4.1 Rejected-attempt consequences (per environment — Repair Pass 4)
For every environment, distinguish these cases:
1. **Invalidity determinable by the source mechanism or source application:** reject before fee or principal moves whenever technically possible.
2. **Invalidity determinable only through the proof-verification path:** reject before the event becomes a recognized Commitment Vault Lock; authorize no Base issuance; create no Reward-Accounting Credit; consume no Handshake allowance.
3. **Arbitrary or out-of-protocol source transaction:** creates no Commitment Vault Lock; creates no issuance right; creates no Reward-Accounting Credit; consumes no Handshake allowance merely because assets moved or were timelocked.

**Over-limit attempt on a non-stateful source mechanism that moves fee or principal before Base rejection (precise consequence):** the attempt is **not recognized** — it creates no Commitment Vault Lock, no Base issuance, no Reward-Accounting Credit, and consumes no Handshake allowance. Principal locked by such an unrecognized attempt **remains deterministically releasable at maturity** under the fixed principal-release rule (independent of Base/relayer/price/verifier/administrator). **Fee treatment (settled — owner-approved rule, Repair Pass 5):** if a fee is actually and irreversibly transferred to the fixed Dev Fund destination, that fee is permanently non-refundable — even if the source transaction is later rejected as an unrecognized or over-limit attempt. Such an attempt does not become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock; authorizes no Base issuance; creates no Reward-Accounting Credit; consumes no Trust-Building Handshake allowance; and creates no other Vinculum protocol rights. Any principal timelocked by the transaction remains deterministically releasable to its bound destination at maturity, independently of Base, verification, relayers, price services, administrators, or later cooperation. This is no longer an open OWNER DECISION (Section I.5 updated); it is included in the proposed Revision 6 language (R.11). The non-refundable rule is a fail-safe for an exceptional or independently constructed transaction; the official application must prevent the fee-loss scenario whenever technically possible (Section Q.4.2).

### Q.4.2 Prevention by the official application (Repair Pass 5)

The non-refundable fee rule (Section Q.4.1) is a fail-safe for an exceptional or independently constructed transaction. It must **not** be treated as an acceptable ordinary User experience. The official application must perform every technically available check before constructing, signing, or broadcasting the source transaction, including: the applicable one-use or three-use Handshake allowance; whether the identity is already used; a pending attempt by the same identity; a recognized attempt awaiting finality; a duplicate submission; the approved asset and identity; the qualifying value range; the fee and principal calculations; output-token availability; and the recipient and release-destination binding. For a non-stateful one-use mechanism, the application must refuse to construct another Handshake transaction while the identity is used or has an unresolved pending attempt. The interface must clearly warn that independently constructing or broadcasting a source transaction can move or timelock assets without creating Vinculum rights and that any fee actually transferred to the Dev Fund is non-refundable. The testing goal (Section Q.7) is to demonstrate that the official application prevents the fee-loss scenario whenever technically possible while preserving deterministic behavior if an independently constructed transaction nevertheless causes it.

### Q.4.3 Pending-attempt lifecycle (Repair Pass 6)

The official application's prevention checks (Section Q.4.2) depend on a deterministic lifecycle for an "unresolved pending attempt." A pending attempt is an official Handshake submission whose objective disposition is not yet finalized. The application must not authorize another official Handshake for a one-use identity while that identity has an objectively pending attempt, and must not rely on application-local timers, mempool observation, or cached state to determine protocol recognition or invalidation.

**Rule — objective disposition only.** Elapsed time, mempool disappearance, failure to appear within an estimated or "finality" window, and application-local abandonment timers are not proof that a transaction can no longer become recognized. A pending attempt may clear the pending state only when objective chain-native evidence establishes either (1) it reached a terminal source-chain disposition, or (2) it can no longer validly become the recognized transaction. Application-local state is preventive only and never consumes protocol allowance. Before authorizing another official submission, the application must recheck both the objective source-chain disposition and the Base recognition state.

**Deterministic lifecycle states (per official attempt):**
1. **Submitted** — the application constructed, signed, and broadcast (or queued for broadcast) the source transaction for a bound identity; the attempt is pending until objective disposition.
2. **Objectively resolved — recognized** — the Base recognition state records the attempt as a recognized Commitment Vault Lock (allowance consumed); the identity's one-use allowance is consumed.
3. **Objectively resolved — not recognized** — the attempt reached a terminal source-chain disposition that is not a recognized lock (reverted, failed, or objectively invalid), or can no longer validly become the recognized transaction (consumed nonce / consumed input / expired finite validity bound); no allowance is consumed; the pending state clears.
4. **Still pending** — no objective evidence of a terminal disposition or of objective invalidation exists; a still-valid, rebroadcastable transaction remains in this state regardless of elapsed time or mempool non-observation. It does not clear by timer.

**Reconciliation rule:** the application reconciles its pending state against (a) objective chain-native evidence (terminal source-chain disposition, conflicting-spend/nonce/sequence consumption, genuine chain-native expiry, finite validity-bound passage) and (b) the Base recognition state. Application-local timers or cached state may advise the User but cannot determine protocol recognition or objective invalidation. A pending attempt is cleared only when objective chain-native evidence establishes a terminal disposition or that the transaction can no longer validly become the recognized transaction; elapsed time, mempool disappearance, or non-observation does not clear a still-valid pending attempt. On application restart/recovery, the application must re-query objective and Base state before authorizing any submission; stale cached state alone cannot authorize an additional recognized Handshake.

**Guarantees preserved:**
- a one-use identity with an objectively pending attempt cannot submit another official Handshake;
- stale application state cannot authorize an additional recognized Handshake (the application re-queries objective + Base state before authorizing);
- a still-valid, rebroadcastable UTXO transaction remains pending and is not cleared by elapsed time, mempool eviction, or non-observation;
- a finalized conflicting UTXO spend (double-spend) objectively clears the original pending attempt (not recognized);
- an EVM same-nonce finalized replacement/cancellation objectively clears the original pending attempt;
- Solana recent-blockhash expiry clears a non-durable-nonce attempt; durable-nonce advancement clears the superseded attempt;
- XRPL finite LastLedgerSequence passage and Stellar finite-time-bound/sequence-consumption objectively clear the original;
- no objectively pending identity receives a second official submission;
- application-local timers or cached state alone cannot determine protocol recognition or objective invalidation;
- concurrent attempts cannot result in more recognized Handshakes than the applicable allowance (recognition is the single arbiter);
- failed, reverted, invalid, dropped, and unrecognized attempts consume no allowance;
- if a fee actually and irreversibly reached the fixed Dev Fund destination, it remains non-refundable under the settled rule (Section Q.4.1);
- principal remains deterministically releasable at maturity.

**Per-mechanism-family pending-resolution criteria:**
- **EVM (all 7):** the source contract atomically consumes the allowance at source completion (recognized event); a reverted/failed attempt rolls back the counter. A pending attempt is resolved when the transaction is finalized as successful (recognized) or reverted (not recognized), or when the same account nonce is consumed by a finalized replacement or cancellation transaction (the original can no longer validly become the recognized transaction; not recognized). Mempool disappearance or elapsed time alone does not resolve it. Pending-resolution is RESOLVED — DEPLOYABLE MECHANISM ESTABLISHED (the on-chain per-identity counter is the arbiter; nonce consumption is objectively observable on a finalized block).
- **Solana:** the source program atomically consumes the allowance at source completion; a failed attempt rolls back the counter. A pending attempt is resolved when the transaction is finalized as successful (recognized) or failed (not recognized), or when its recent blockhash objectively expires without inclusion (not recognized). A durable-nonce transaction remains pending until it is finalized or the durable nonce is advanced by a finalized transaction (the superseded attempt is then not recognized). Mempool/leader non-observation or elapsed time alone does not resolve a transaction whose blockhash/nonce remains valid. Pending-resolution is RESOLVED — DEPLOYABLE MECHANISM ESTABLISHED.
- **Non-stateful UTXO-family mechanisms (Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dogecoin, DigiByte, Zcash, Bitcoin Cash):** recognition and the one-use allowance are determined at Base recognition, not at source. A pending attempt is resolved when (i) the source transaction is finalized and its canonical release public key's lock is recognized by Base (recognized; allowance consumed), or (ii) the source transaction becomes objectively invalid — a conflicting transaction spending one or more of the same inputs is finalized (double-spend; the original can no longer validly become the recognized transaction; not recognized) — or, if and only if that chain actually provides a genuine chain-native expiry rule that invalidates the transaction, that expiry is reached (not recognized). Mempool eviction, non-observation, elapsed time, or failure to confirm within an estimated window does not invalidate a still-valid UTXO transaction; if it remains valid and rebroadcastable, it remains pending. No automatic cancellation mechanism is invented. The application must not submit another Handshake for the same canonical release public key while the prior attempt's source transaction is not yet objectively resolved. Pending-resolution criteria (finality depth, conflicting-spend/double-spend detection, genuine chain-native expiry if any, reorg handling) are DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED (tied to the deployable on-Base verifier, Section O); not an OWNER DECISION.
- **XRP Ledger:** a pending attempt requires a finite `LastLedgerSequence` bound. It is resolved when the transaction reaches a validated-ledger disposition (recognized or failed) or when that `LastLedgerSequence` passes without the transaction being validated (it can no longer validly become the recognized transaction; not recognized). Elapsed time or mempool non-observation alone does not resolve it before the finite bound passes. Pending-resolution is DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED (tied to the deployable verifier and atomic design; the finite `LastLedgerSequence` requirement is a recognized-design constraint).
- **Stellar:** a pending attempt requires finite time bounds. It is resolved when the transaction is accepted (recognized) or fails (not recognized) in a closed ledger, or when the maximum time bound passes without inclusion, or when the relevant source-account sequence number is consumed by a finalized transaction so the original transaction is no longer valid (not recognized). Elapsed time alone does not resolve it before the finite bound. Pending-resolution is DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED.
- **Cosmos Hub:** pending-resolution is EVIDENCE REQUIRED — CHAIN-NATIVE FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS INCOMPLETE (dependent on the unresolved mechanism).

A pending attempt that has reached no objective terminal disposition and is not objectively invalid remains pending; it does not clear by application-local timer, mempool eviction, or non-observation. Only objective terminal disposition or objective invalidation (consumed nonce, consumed input by a finalized conflicting transaction, genuine chain-native expiry, or finite validity-bound passage) clears the pending state.

### Q.5 CODA's engineering authority
Before marking an item OWNER DECISION REQUIRED, CODA must: (1) identify the required outcome or invariant; (2) examine the actual native capabilities of that chain; (3) design one or more chain-native mechanisms intended to achieve the outcome; (4) verify that all required opcodes, amendments, transaction types, programs, proofs, and consensus features genuinely exist and are deployable; (5) test the design against partial completion, replay, concurrency, false evidence, early release, redirection, dependency failure, and principal-loss cases; (6) explain precisely why every compliant candidate fails if no compliant mechanism can be established. **A summary statement that a module requires governance is not, by itself, the required evidence package.** EVIDENCE REQUIRED — CHAIN-NATIVE FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS INCOMPLETE is used when authoritative capability evidence, candidate mechanisms, deployability verification, or invariant-failure testing is incomplete. OWNER DECISION REQUIRED is used only when the complete analysis shows no chain-native design can satisfy the outcome without changing economics, User rights, trust assumptions, security, supported scope, or principal safety, or the Master Specification genuinely requires a new protocol rule. Under this authority, **Cosmos Hub is classified EVIDENCE REQUIRED — CHAIN-NATIVE FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS INCOMPLETE** (Section C.12) pending the complete six-step analysis; it is not OWNER DECISION in this pass.

### Q.6 Status terminology (Repair Pass 4)
Do not use an unqualified RESOLVED status merely because a generic CLTV pattern, batch, verifier, counter, module, or proof path has been named. Distinguish:
- **DESIGN DEFINED — DEPLOYABILITY EVIDENCE REQUIRED** — the design is defined but a deployable mechanism/verifier is not yet established.
- **RESOLVED — DEPLOYABLE MECHANISM ESTABLISHED** — a deployable, self-contained mechanism is genuinely established.
A Base recognition counter for a non-stateful mechanism may be design-defined while its deployable verifier remains EVIDENCE REQUIRED. The reduction from three uses to one does not, by itself, establish that the proof-verification path is deployed or proven.

## R. Proposed Revision 6 amendment language (Master Specification synchronization — Repair Pass 4)

The chain-equivalent outcome principle, the settled per-identity interpretation, and the one-versus-three Handshake rule are **owner-approved protocol directions that are not yet fully incorporated into the current governing Revision 5**. This architecture pass does **not** claim that Revision 5 already contains them, and does **not** modify any governing input. The following is exact proposed language for a narrow future **Revision 6** amendment; it is a writable deliverable only as proposed text, not an enacted specification change.

> **R.1 Chain-equivalent outcomes.** The protocol requires equivalent outcomes, security, and economic performance across supported source environments. It does not require identical contracts, transaction structures, state models, or implementation methods. A chain-native mechanism is evaluated by whether it produces the required observable result, not by resemblance to an EVM contract.
>
> **R.2 Handshake allowance by source-mechanism state capability.** The Trust-Building Handshake allowance depends on the selected source mechanism's chain-native state capability: (a) a source mechanism capable of atomically maintaining persistent per-identity allowance state permits three successful qualifying Trust-Building Handshakes per objectively bound source-chain identity; (b) a source mechanism without that capability permits one successful qualifying Trust-Building Handshake per objectively bound source-chain identity. The applicable allowance is determined by the actual selected source mechanism, not by a broad chain label.
>
> **R.3 Objective identity.** The Handshake identity is the objectively recorded source-chain identity chosen per source environment and deterministically bound into the recognized lock evidence. For each UTXO-family mechanism exactly one canonical release public key is selected (R.13).
>
> **R.4 New identities.** Each newly created, objectively distinct wallet, account, or canonical release public key (UTXO-family mechanisms, R.13) receives its own applicable allowance. Creating another wallet, account, or release public key is permitted and is not a bypass, security defect, Sybil issue, or prohibited behavior.
>
> **R.5 Prohibition on person-level enforcement.** No person-level identification, wallet clustering, cross-address association, detection of common control, or anti-Sybil enforcement is required or permitted.
>
> **R.6 Failed attempts.** Failed, reverted, invalid, and unrecognized attempts consume no Handshake allowance.
>
> **R.7 One-use mechanism.** For a one-use mechanism, the first successful qualifying Handshake consumes the identity's allowance; a second qualifying attempt by the same identity cannot become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock or authorize issuance; a newly created distinct identity may complete its own one permitted Handshake.
>
> **R.8 Three-use mechanism.** For a three-use mechanism, the first three successful qualifying Handshakes consume the identity's allowance; the fourth qualifying attempt by the same identity is rejected.
>
> **R.9 Proof-verification-path qualification and allowance enforcement.** Preflight qualification and allowance enforcement may be placed at the source, in a cryptographic authorization required by the source mechanism, in the proof-verification path, or across a chain-native combination, provided the resulting User-protection and security outcome are demonstrated. An invalidity determinable only through the proof-verification path is rejected before the event becomes a recognized Commitment Vault Lock and authorizes no issuance, no Reward-Accounting Credit, and consumes no Handshake allowance.
>
> **R.10 Out-of-protocol transactions.** An arbitrary transaction constructed outside the protocol does not become a Commitment Vault Lock and creates no issuance right, no Reward-Accounting Credit, and no Handshake-allowance consumption merely because it moves or timelocks assets.
>
> **R.11 Settled fee rule.** If a fee is actually and irreversibly transferred to the fixed Dev Fund destination, that fee is permanently non-refundable — even if the source transaction is later rejected as an unrecognized or over-limit attempt. Such an attempt does not become a recognized Commitment Vault Lock; authorizes no Base issuance; creates no Reward-Accounting Credit; consumes no Trust-Building Handshake allowance; and creates no other Vinculum protocol rights. Any principal timelocked by the transaction remains deterministically releasable to its bound destination at maturity, independently of Base, verification, relayers, price services, administrators, or later cooperation.
>
> **R.12 Prevention by the official application.** The non-refundable rule is a fail-safe for an exceptional or independently constructed transaction, not an acceptable ordinary User experience. The official application must perform every technically available check before constructing, signing, or broadcasting the source transaction — including the applicable Handshake allowance, an already-used identity, a pending attempt by the same identity, a recognized attempt awaiting finality, duplicate submission, approved asset and identity, qualifying value range, fee and principal calculations, output-token availability, and recipient and release-destination binding. For a non-stateful one-use mechanism, the application must refuse to construct another Handshake transaction while the identity is used or has an unresolved pending attempt. The interface must clearly warn that independently constructing or broadcasting a source transaction can move or timelock assets without creating Vinculum rights and that any fee actually transferred to the Dev Fund is non-refundable.
>
> **R.13 Canonical release public key.** For each UTXO-family source mechanism the Handshake identity is exactly one canonical release public key: `handshake_identity = (source_environment_id, canonical_release_public_key)`, where the canonical release public key is the single public key authorizing the principal's maturity-release branch, serialized in the chain's canonical public-key form and bound/proved against the principal output's locking script, redeem/witness script, or tapleaf evidence. The address, complete script, script hash, and maturity value are not used as alternative identities. Handshake-compatible UTXO outputs must expose exactly one unambiguous maturity-release public key; ambiguous or multi-key maturity-release constructions are rejected for Handshake use. The canonical release public key is singular per recognized lock, deterministically derivable from the principal timelock output, objectively provable from the source transaction, bound into the normalized evidence, and used consistently by the Base one-use recognition counter. A newly generated release public key remains permitted and gives that objectively distinct identity its own one-use allowance.

> **R.14 Objective pending-attempt disposition.** Elapsed time, mempool disappearance, failure to appear within an estimated or "finality" window, and application-local abandonment timers are not proof that a source transaction can no longer become recognized. A pending Trust-Building Handshake attempt clears the pending state only when objective chain-native evidence establishes a terminal source-chain disposition or that the transaction can no longer validly become the recognized transaction — consumed account nonce; consumed input by a finalized conflicting transaction; a genuine chain-native expiry rule where the chain provides one; or a finite validity bound (such as XRPL `LastLedgerSequence` or Stellar finite time bounds) that passes. A still-valid, rebroadcastable UTXO transaction remains pending and is not cleared by time, mempool eviction, or non-observation. Application-local state is preventive only and never consumes protocol allowance.

This proposed language is the complete narrow Revision 6 amendment recommended by this architecture pass (Repair Pass 5 adds R.11—R.13; Repair Pass 6A adds R.14). Enactment is an owner/specification action outside this pass.
