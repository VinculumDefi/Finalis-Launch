# Vinculum Product Design Charter
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Accepted Baseline

---

## Origins

This charter emerged from the completion of the protocol construction phase, the acceptance of Presentation Map A (Commitment Lifecycle), Presentation Map B (Participation Lifecycle), the Product Information Architecture, and the Product Architecture Index. It captures the enduring product design philosophy derived from those artifacts and serves as the decision framework for future product work.

---

## Purpose

The Master Specification defines **what Vinculum is**.

The Presentation Maps define **how participants experience the protocol**.

The Product Information Architecture organizes **what the product must present**.

This charter defines **how every product decision shall be made**.

It governs the design of the Vinculum website, application, dashboards, documentation, educational material, and public-facing experiences. It does not modify the Master Specification or establish protocol requirements.

---

# Our Objective

Vinculum is not designed to persuade people to trust it.

It is designed to help people understand it.

The product exists to progressively replace uncertainty with understanding, understanding with evidence, and evidence with independent verification.

---

# North Star

> **The product succeeds when a participant no longer relies on the application to tell them the protocol is working, because they have learned how to verify it themselves.**

**Competence—not dependence—is the destination.**

---

# Design Acceptance Test

Every page, workflow, dashboard, visualization, document, educational experience, and feature shall be evaluated by one question:

> **Does this help the participant become more capable of understanding and independently verifying the protocol?**

**If the answer is no, it does not belong.**

---

# Design Principles

## 1. The protocol informs. The participant decides.

The product shall provide information, evidence, and understanding.

Authority remains with the participant.

## 2. Teach before asking for action.

Concepts should appear only after the participant has the knowledge required to understand them.

Understanding always precedes commitment.

## 3. Replace uncertainty with evidence.

Whenever uncertainty exists, the product should help participants discover objective evidence rather than offering reassurance.

Evidence should always be preferred over assertion.

## 4. Build confidence through verification.

Confidence should emerge naturally as participants successfully verify the protocol for themselves.

The application should never ask someone to trust what they can instead verify.

## 5. Make complexity understandable—not invisible.

The protocol should never be oversimplified by hiding important mechanics.

Instead, complexity should be introduced progressively, when it becomes relevant.

## 6. Preserve participant responsibility.

The product should make important decisions understandable.

It should not quietly make those decisions on behalf of participants.

Convenience must never replace informed participation.

## 7. Tell the truth about limitations.

The product shall communicate risks, constraints, delays, and accepted limitations honestly.

Clarity builds trust.

Concealment destroys it.

## 8. Stay faithful to the protocol.

Product decisions must faithfully express the Master Specification.

Presentation may improve understanding.

Presentation must never change protocol meaning.

# Success

The product has succeeded when participants can independently answer:

- What happens to my asset?
- Why did this result occur?
- How was this amount calculated?
- How do I verify it?
- Where is the evidence?
- What happens if part of the system fails?

…without relying on the project team's explanation.

---

# Final Principle

The purpose of the Vinculum product is not to become indispensable.

Its purpose is to help participants become knowledgeable, confident, and independently capable users of the protocol.

When the participant no longer needs to trust the application because they understand how to verify the protocol themselves, the product has fulfilled its purpose.

---

# Revision Policy

This charter should evolve rarely.

Revisions require evidence that a principle no longer faithfully supports the Master Specification, the accepted Presentation Maps, or the participant experience they define.
